Protests began on 28 December 2017 in Mashhad, Iran's second-largest city, over rising prices, unemployment and corruption, and spread to more than 100 cities into mid-January 2018. Amnesty International says demonstrators also spoke out against political repression. The period is known as the Dey protests, after the Persian month in which it began.

<!--more-->

Human Rights Watch reported on 9 January 2018 that MP Mahmoud Sadeghi said 3,700 people had been arrested. Amnesty International noted that the judiciary spokesperson said on 14 January 2018 that 25 people had been killed, while stating the real number may be higher. Human Rights Watch also reported at least 21 deaths and deaths in detention in Tehran and Arak.

Amnesty International said three years later that no criminal investigation had been opened into the killings. At least one protester, Mostafa Salehi, was executed in August 2020 in connection with the protests.
