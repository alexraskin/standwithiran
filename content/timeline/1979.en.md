Protests against Shah Mohammad Reza Pahlavi grew through 1978. Britannica attributes them to opposition to autocracy, economic difficulties, sociopolitical repression and Western interference. On 8 September 1978, troops fired on demonstrators in Tehran under martial law, killing, Britannica says, dozens or hundreds. Strikes followed, including by oil workers, who halted the oil industry.

<!--more-->

The Shah left Iran in January 1979. Ayatollah Ruhollah Khomeini returned on 1 February, and on 11 February the armed forces declared neutrality, ending the monarchy. On 1 April, after a referendum, Khomeini declared Iran an Islamic Republic. A constitution adopted later that year centred power on a supreme leader, first Khomeini himself.

In November 1979, Iranian students seized the US Embassy in Tehran, holding, the US Office of the Historian records, more than 50 Americans hostage for 444 days. The new clerical leadership also sidelined left-wing and centrist allies who had opposed the Shah, consolidating the system that still governs Iran.
