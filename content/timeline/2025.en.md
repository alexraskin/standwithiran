Protests began on 28 December 2025, amid economic hardship and soaring prices, according to the UN Special Rapporteur, and became a nationwide movement demanding political change. The UN Fact-Finding Mission found killings by state security forces in all 31 provinces, with the highest tolls in Tehran, Karaj, Mashhad, Isfahan and Rasht.

<!--more-->

On 8 January 2026, as the crackdown intensified, authorities imposed a nationwide internet and communications blackout. The Fact-Finding Mission said it lasted over five months, the longest shutdown on record. No verified death toll exists: Iranian authorities reported over 3,000 deaths, while some civil society estimates reached tens of thousands, which UN officials said could not be verified because of the shutdown. The Mission reported at least 221 children killed and tens of thousands arrested.

Between March and August 2026, the Mission said, at least 29 men were executed after expedited trials in protest-related cases. It concluded many violations amounted to crimes against humanity.
