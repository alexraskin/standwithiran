Between late July and September 1988, Iranian authorities removed political prisoners from their cells and brought them before panels Amnesty International calls "death commissions", involving judicial, prosecution and intelligence officials. Prisoners were asked to renounce their beliefs and political groups. Many who gave answers deemed incorrect were hanged or shot by firing squad.

<!--more-->

Most victims were supporters of the People's Mojahedin Organization of Iran (PMOI). Hundreds affiliated with leftist and Kurdish groups were also killed. Amnesty International put minimum estimates at around 5,000 deaths in its 2018 report, *Blood-soaked Secrets*. Its earlier 1990 report, hosted by the Abdorrahman Boroumand Center, had recorded the names of over 2,000 reportedly executed prisoners.

Bodies were placed in unmarked individual and mass graves, and most families were not told where. Amnesty International says no official has been brought to justice, and it has called for an independent UN investigation.
